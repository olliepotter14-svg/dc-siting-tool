Level 1 · Headline

The pre-reform queue was 3.7× what the UK needs by 2030 — NESO's Gate 2 readiness test removed 244 GW of zombie projects

The Existing Agreements (EA) Register replaces the TEC queue as the authoritative list of connection projects that have demonstrated deliverability. Our siting tool's queue-pressure scores now draw from it directly.

738GW
Pre-reform total queue — TEC Register applications, early 2024. 3.7× the UK's 2030 capacity target.
494GW
EA Register (consented) — Gate-2 successful + grandfathered agreements. The source we now score against.
244GW
"Zombie" pool removed — demoted to Gate 1 holding, withdrawn, or excluded from the register entirely.
49GW
Demand-side capacity — 119 projects (data centres, hydrogen, industry) now visible nationally for the first time.
Why this matters

The old TEC queue was so bloated with speculative entries that every substation looked saturated — "queue pressure" was an information-free metric. The EA Register restores the signal: a substation's queue MW now reflects projects that have actually proved they can deliver under NESO's new readiness test. Substations whose queue pressure looks high in the new model are high for real, not for show.

So what

The reform was effective but not over-aggressive: the consented EA Register is still ~2.5× UK 2030 demand. That's what our siting tool now answers using the right denominator.

Caveat

The 29 GW "Connection Nodes" bucket isn't a failure of parsing — it's by design. NESO created multi-tenant nodes ("Power Park Rugby", "Trent Valley North Connection Node B", "GEC Ethos Green") to host a cluster of downstream uses at one connection point. True demand-type splits require cross-referencing planning applications or operator press releases per project — the EA Register on its own doesn't decompose them.

Pin for the room

Explicitly named data centres total 8.8 GW across 18 EA-Register projects — the floor. If 30% of the 29 GW of Connection Node capacity ultimately connects DC load (a defensible mid-case), total queued DC demand approaches 17.5 GW — comparable to the entire 2024 UK DC build-out base.
